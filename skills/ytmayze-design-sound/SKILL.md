# @ytmayze Video Sound Design

Sound gives edits weight, depth and comedy. Choose sounds for the action and emotional purpose, rather than adding a generic pop/riser to every transition. Game-like and meme sounds are a useful vocabulary when the context fits; no named meme sound is a universal signature.

## Establish the audio hierarchy

Distinguish tutorial speech, DAW note auditions/beat playback, performance vocals, meme source audio and added editorial cues. First identify what the viewer should hear. A sound already in the beat is not automatically an extra editorial cue to duplicate. Avoid competing music beds and protect recurring melody levels and intentional processing.

Use the current approved source/mix as the anchor. If mouse clicks overpower notes, lower the added clicks rather than raising the melody and making its later recurrence inconsistent. Do not process an entire mixed recording to solve one dialogue section. Match speech perceptually and gently; heavy restoration/boosting of distant speech previously sounded cheap and brittle. Keep protected beat/freestyle/effected voice sections intact.

Before conforming replacement/cleaned audio, check whether picture cuts are still changing. If so, defer the final conform where practical or segment/link replacement audio to the same edit units; avoid a long unrelated layer that must be manually recut after every picture change. Verify source offsets, seams, ripple behavior and absence of doubled original audio.

## Foley follows the action

Place mouse sounds on visible button presses or note-entry actions, not on every musical note onset. Inspect existing cues first: adjust or replace them where needed rather than duplicating a sound on an already served action. For a drag, represent the gesture with a fitting grab/down, held movement if useful, and release; a lone ordinary click may misdescribe it. Retain an already approved drag sound when the task only concerns nearby clicks.

Vary level and, where useful, samples within one mouse/keyboard identity. Variation should be audible enough to avoid mechanical repetition yet sound like the same device. Choose levels by listening against the notes, not by a universal dB setting. Preserve the music level when adjusting editorial cues. For assets with pre-roll or delayed impact, align the audible event rather than just the asset start.

## Sound as a storytelling tool

Give a joke’s source audio room when its voice or rhythm is the punchline. If a pop-up sequence escalates to a crash/reveal, develop the cue sequence and final payoff; repeating one sound at one level may make it flat. A deliberately dry silence can be stronger than another accent.

Use rises, reverse tails, stingers, interruption sounds or a short impact only where they prepare or explain the next section. Keep tails from masking the next line or musical reveal. If temporary bed ducking is justified for an approved gag, keep it local and restore it cleanly; do not use general ducking to solve an overloaded edit. For an actual plugin demo, keep audition audio synchronized to the visible selection; speed up loading separately from the audible preview.

## Review

Preserve cuts and source timing unless explicitly reopened. Make project edits in a new editable copy. Check added cue boundaries, action sync, peaks/clipping, duplicate audio, transitions and the return to the existing mix. Listen continuously with the section before/after, including repeated melodies. Waveforms and successful decode do not prove balance, quality or comic timing. Say when listening remains pending.
