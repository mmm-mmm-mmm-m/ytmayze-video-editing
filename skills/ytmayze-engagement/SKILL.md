# @ytmayze Memes and On-Screen Engagement

Combine tutorial clarity with entertainment, dry humor and occasional deliberate absurdity. The viewer should feel the edit’s reason even when the image is surprising. The style is not a requirement for constant memes, random overlays or a fixed set of characters.

## Make the gag earn its place

Inspect what is said, shown and heard before and after the candidate. Name its job: emphasize a sound, dramatize a challenge, rescue a flat delivery, heighten a reaction, or bridge two worlds. Choose a treatment whose reference or absurdity helps that job. “Alien transmissions” does not by itself justify any alien visual; a literal collage was later rejected as out of place.

Give the joke an entry, recognizable action and payoff, then a clear return. A spoken challenger needs its own intelligible space rather than talking over the creator. With locked cuts, fit it into an existing suitable slot or use another device. Do not silently lengthen the timeline. Evaluate a hold in context: the Tung payoff became too short after a trim, so neither “shorter is better” nor a fixed meme duration is a rule.

Surprise can be paid off through sound, a matched action or intentional silence. A small slow zoom may support awkwardness better than aggressive wiggle. If a sequence builds toward a crash, escalate the visuals and audio so the crash resolves something. Avoid adding decoration merely because a quiet passage exists.

## Compose typography for its role

For ordinary process labels and reaction captions, start from the project’s accepted font, stroke/shadow, margins and placement. Match neighboring examples rather than mixing unrelated styles. Use text when it adds information or a joke; remove redundant “full beat”/“final touches” labels when the picture and sound carry it.

A spoken or performed shortcut can justify the reusable laptop showcase: real FL screen on the laptop, relevant key highlights, explanatory text and synchronized sound. It can add value and refresh pacing when it fits the demonstration. Do not fill every quiet gap with it. Use `ytmayze-effect-presets`, if available, for the editable adapter and verify the shortcut against the actual platform/action.

A freestyle/lyric treatment can intentionally use large stretched words, planned line breaks, pulse, pixel texture or full-frame composition when that is the chosen reference. Do not apply that look to all explanatory captions. Read the actual lyric and vocal timing; automated transcript timing is a starting point. Keep words readable long enough for their role.

Center the visible lettering and its balanced margins, not only a layer’s anchor coordinates. Match the reference’s apparent scale and negative space; scale down a word that crowds the frame. Different words need not occupy identical width and height. Preserve glyph proportions unless an intentional stretched-lyric treatment is requested. For a typography-only task, change only the requested properties; centering/scaling alone does not authorize a font replacement. Preserve text timing, pumps, cuts and audio. Inspect all instances and animation extremes.

## Cards and transitions

Make a CTA an integrated visual beat with purposeful placement, entrance and a fitting sound. An in-tutorial card usually needs an exit; an ending card may hold. Keep relevant UI unobstructed and remove unnecessary blank card space. If the user requests a mock first, deliver the mock before integrating it. Use current supplied/verified offer copy, never an old trial or price inferred from the Saint Alien ending.

Real product UI and actual actions should remain authentic; stylized scenery and game imagery may support them. Keep the viewer able to understand that a playful product bridge leads to a real tool. Do not turn a stylistic request into automatic face swaps or reuse of an unrelated movie clip.

Apply authorized edits in an editable copy, respecting locked timing. Review the gag continuously with its source audio and the surrounding performance. Treat a technically successful render as a draft until the relevant creative review occurs. Read [engagement examples](references/examples.md) when adapting an older motif.
